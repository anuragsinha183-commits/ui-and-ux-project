Redesigning Zomato for Frictionless Dining & Transparent Ordering

An empirical UX analysis identifying information overload, hidden bill shock, and group ordering friction in Zomato, backed by 3 high-impact redesign solutions and visual mockups.

👤 Author: Anurag Sinha 📱 Platform: Mobile iOS & Android ⏱ Scope: Core Food Discovery & Checkout Flow

01 User Journey & Personas

Zomato is an industry benchmark for Indian food tech, but years of aggressive cross-selling (Gold, Live Events, Blinkit) have eroded the speed of its primary task: ordering a meal in under 2 minutes.

The Busy Office Professional

Goal: Wants to re-order lunch in under 60 seconds with reliable 25-minute delivery.

Pain Point: Overwhelmed by flashing event ads and 10+ banner carousels before reaching food items.

The Family / Group Planner

Goal: Order for 4–6 family members with dietary preferences and clear split costs.

Pain Point: Forced to pass phone around or manually juggle orders across WhatsApp chats.

Journey Stage User Mindset Emotional State UX Friction
1. Discovery "What should I have for lunch?" 😐 Neutral / Overwhelmed Banner clutter, auto-playing video promotions.
2. Menu Selection "Which biryani has the best value?" 😕 Hesitant Hidden costs not reflected in card price.
3. Cart Review "Why did my $8 dish become $14.50?" 😠 Frustrated Surprise platform & packaging fees at checkout.
4. Tracking "Where is my rider right now?" 😃 Delighted Smooth real-time GPS telemetry and status updates.

02 Heuristic Evaluation: Strengths vs. Weaknesses

✅ Key UX Strengths

  • • Visibility of System Status: Industry-leading delivery tracker with live courier bike animation and rain radar.
  • • Emotional Branding: Engaging notification copy and witty brand voice that builds brand loyalty.
  • • 1-Tap Reorder Carousel: High-performing memory retrieval for past favorite orders.

❌ Critical UX Weaknesses

  • • Hick's Law Violation: Too many competing banner choices and ads induce choice paralysis.
  • • Surprise Bill Shock: Platform fees, surge packaging fees, and taxes hidden until the final payment step.
  • • Lack of Group Ordering: No native collaborative cart or bill split for teams and families.

03 3 Proposed UX Solutions & Redesigns

1. "QuickMode" Zero-Clutter Home Toggle

Introduce a prominent toggle switch on the home header. When enabled, it suppresses all entertainment, gold banners, and sponsored ads, leaving only 3 essential components: 1-Tap Re-Order strip, Top 5 Local Comfort Foods, and Under-25-Min Delivery Guarantee.

UX Principle: Hick's Law & Flexibility of Use (Nielsen #7)

2. "True-Price" Upfront Bill Transparency

Eliminate checkout bill shock by displaying all-inclusive estimated totals directly under the listed item price (e.g. "$8.99 — All-Inclusive: $10.45 incl. taxes & packaging"). No surprise charges appear at the payment step.

UX Principle: Match Between System & Real World (Nielsen #2)

3. Real-Time Collaborative "Group Cart & Split"

A native collaborative cart where a host generates a shared link/QR code via WhatsApp. All participants add their meals and special notes in real time from their own phones, with automatic bill calculation per person.

UX Principle: Recognition over Recall & Social UX
